# Of Folk and Many Winters — Implementation Status

Progress against [`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md), one entry per roadmap step, in the order the steps were actually worked on. Each entry records what exists today, how it is wired up, and what was deliberately deferred. This is the detailed engineering log; the high-level overview lives in the repository [`README.md`](../README.md).


- **Step 2 — Simulation skeleton**: a simulation clock, stable person IDs, a minimal `Person`/`Needs`/task-queue model, and JSON-based save/load, all in `ManyWinters.Core` with no Godot dependency. The task-queue (`PersonTaskQueue`/`PersonTask`) existed since this step but sat unused until now: `WorldState.Advance` drives each alive person's current task every tick, and `MoveTask` is the first real one — it nudges `Person.Position` toward a destination by a fixed speed per tick and completes on arrival, rather than teleporting. `MoveCommand` issues one, pre-empting whatever the person was doing (`PersonTaskQueue.Interrupt`) rather than queuing behind it. In Godot, clicking empty ground with a person selected issues the move; their sprite glides smoothly toward the new position every rendered frame (`PersonView`'s own interpolation, at whatever speed covers the actual distance the simulation moved them in exactly one tick) rather than snapping once per tick. Every action command with an actual spatial target — gather, construct, repair, deposit, withdraw, bury, teach — now requires the acting person be within `SimulationRules.MaxInteractionDistance` (a single shared rule, checked via `WorldState.IsWithinReach`) of it, silently no-op'ing otherwise just like every other invalid-command case already did; `craft` is the one exception, since it only touches a person's own inventory and has no location to be near. The Godot buttons that pick a target automatically (repair/deposit/withdraw/bury's "nearest") now say so explicitly ("The nearest building is too far away.") instead of silently doing nothing, and auto-picked build placement is kept within range by construction rather than by luck.
- **Step 3 — Headless runner**: `generate`, `create <n>`, `simulate <ticks>`, `print population`, `save`/`load` — runs the simulation with no Godot involved.
- **Step 4 — Godot presentation**: a "Spawn Person" button goes through a real `ICommand` (`SpawnPersonCommand`) instead of touching simulation state directly, and the placeholder capsules this step started with are long gone: people, resources, buildings and graves are billboarded sprites (`BillboardSprite`) drawn from the pixel art `art/generate_sprites.py` generates. Every billboard uses FixedY rather than full/spherical billboarding, so a sprite's own bottom edge lands at its real world-space height instead of floating at this camera's oblique tilt. `WorldPresenter` is the only thing that ever builds a view, one per entity, off `WorldState`'s own added-entity events. All four view families (`PersonView`, `ResourceNodeView`, `GraveView`, `BuildingView`) sit on one base, `SpriteEntityView`: it owns the sprite layers, the ground shadow, the seeded scale and its ground-contact correction, the collision shape cut to the drawn silhouette rather than the square canvas, hover, click routing and the fog fade, while each view supplies only what genuinely differs — which layers it draws out of which textures, what its seed varies, what a click on it means, and any animation of its own (only `PersonView` has one, a walk cycle). That base exists because the same code had been written out four times and the copies had drifted: graves and buildings never dimmed with the fog at all, buildings alone had no collision shape, a hovered sprite's click rectangle stayed the size it was before hover grew it, and a person could be picked only by their body and never by the cloak hanging off it. A view that nothing can point at or click yet (a building) is created without a hover arbiter or a missed-click handler, which is what makes the base skip its collision shape and ray picking entirely.

  Pointing at things is pixel-accurate rather than shape-approximate, because a round canopy touches most of its square canvas and no box or capsule can stand in for it: `SpritePixelHit` reconstructs where the pick ray crosses the sprite's actual rendered plane (the geometry itself is engine-free in `BillboardUv`) and samples the texture's own alpha there. Because Godot only ever delivers a picking event to the nearest collider along the ray, `HoverRescue` re-casts the same ray past whatever just ruled itself out, so a click and a hover at one screen point always agree on what is really there. `HoverArbiter` holds the single hovered target — there is one cursor, so at most one thing can be lit — and re-asks it once a frame whether the cursor is still on it, which is what stopped highlights sticking on people who simply walked out from under a resting cursor. What being hovered looks like is an inked rim traced along the inside of the entity's own silhouette (`HoverOutline`, `Content/effects/sprite_highlight.gdshader`): one extra pass over its topmost layer, tracing the union of its layers' alpha — they share a quad and a UV mapping, so that union is just the largest alpha any of them has at a UV — dilated by a fixed two screen pixels so the line keeps its weight at any distance. Inward rather than outward, because a canopy touches its own canvas edges and an outward rim would be clipped exactly where the shape is widest, and painted over the art rather than multiplied into it, because Modulate cannot brighten near-black crosshatch ink. It replaced a size bump plus a tint: growing the thing under the cursor moved it out of its own click rectangle, and the tint washed out the drawing it was meant to point at. Sprites standing between the camera and the selection are ghosted every frame (`Main.UpdateOcclusionFade` over `BillboardSprite`'s registry of live billboards, which also spares the per-frame scene-tree walk that scan used to be), a tree's trunk excepted; what the player can see through they can also click and hover through. The selected person wears a screen-space marker anchored to `SpriteEntityView.TopHeightOffset`, the real top of the drawn silhouette rather than a nominal half-height.

  Fog of war has the three tiers the visual plan asked for. `ExplorationState` (Core, 2.5 m cells, a 15 m sight radius per living person, explored staying true forever once set) is the record; the presentation layer reads it through one lens, `RevealableExploration`, whose "Reveal Map" toggle in the inspector is a debugging view that never touches the simulation's own record. `ExplorationMasks` turns it into the sharp per-texel masks `FogOfWarRenderer` feeds its shaders, which multiply sharp by blurred so the soft falloff can only ever show on the unexplored side, and `CloudScatter`/`CloudFogMask`/`GroundClouds` put a drawn cloud layer over the unknown. Every view dims to `RememberedFade.Tint` when the group loses sight of where it stands and brightens back when it regains it — over about a second out, a third of that back, because losing sight is memory gradually taking over while regaining it is an event. A resource node outside the ever-explored area gets no Godot view at all rather than a hidden one, which is what keeps start-up affordable now that decorations are real entities — creating a view for all of them up front was itself the single biggest chunk of start-up time. Of the 17,481 resource nodes a fresh world holds, only those whose cells the group has actually seen exist as nodes at all; the whole running scene sits at roughly 1,465 Godot objects (`Performance.OBJECT_COUNT`, everything included) on a fresh start.

  The sky behind all of it is painted, not simulated (`SkySetup`, `Content/effects/sky.gdshader`): a flat-ish gradient with a few high wisps, no sun disk and no committed time of day, since everything in front of it is a woodcut. Its colours live in `SkyPalette`, which the fog sheet's far edge shares — the two meet along the skyline, and any difference between them would draw a line across the whole map. The sky deliberately does not light the scene: lighting stays the one `DirectionalLight3D` it has always been.

  Deliberately deferred: the ground shadow is one shared round blob for everything instead of each sprite's own silhouette; graves and buildings get the remembered tint but not the "no view until the cell is explored" gate resource nodes have, because nothing the group builds can stand in unexplored ground until the world outlives a nation (see `docs/todo/todo.md`).
- **Step 5 — Environment and survival** (partial): hunger increases every tick and can kill a person, and a person carrying food eats it themselves once hunger has actually built up (`SimulationRules.HungerEatThreshold`, checked through `WorldState.IsHungryEnoughToEat` by both the autonomous pass and `GatherCommand`'s eating straight from the source) rather than taking a bite every tick — a meal is an occasional event, and eating down to zero once is what ends it; resource nodes (one of the resource kinds defined under `src/ManyWinters.Godot/Content/resources/`, e.g. apple/pear/mushroom/potato) can be gathered from — select a person, then click a node to issue a `GatherCommand` — and the node disappears once depleted. Each resource's world height, and the fallback colour its sprite falls back to when the art is missing, come from a `ResourceVisualDefinition.tres` sitting next to its `.json` definition, not from code. Weather/fire/shelter are intentionally deferred to their own later steps. Terrain is real and now wired into the live game (not just a separate sandbox — see [`terrain-and-world-scale-architecture.md`](terrain-and-world-scale-architecture.md) for the full design): a real elevation grid — one 1 km × 1 km, 25 m-cell patch of actual EU-DEM data centered on 50.114722°N 14.4675°E, fetched once via `art/fetch_terrain.py` (the same one-off-script role `generate_sprites.py` plays for pixel art) into a static `Content/terrain/praha-liben/heightmap.json` the game loads locally, never over the network — renders as a real triangulated, textured 3D mesh (`TerrainRenderer`, shared by `Main.cs` and the standalone `TerrainSandbox.tscn` art/camera playground), complete with real OpenStreetMap waterway geometry (`art/fetch_stream.py`) and a scattered tree/rock backdrop. `Position` went from `float` to `double` for this — every entity's coordinate is now real-world meters — with a `FreeCameraRig` (WASD/arrows pan, Q/E rotate, R/F zoom, `T` toggles orthographic/perspective — perspective is the default, settled by direct comparison) replacing the old fixed camera, and `MapLoader.LoadDefault`'s camp relocated onto dry ground away from the real waterway. `WorldPresenter` samples the real terrain height so every entity — people, resource nodes, buildings, graves — actually sits on the slope instead of floating at a flat `y = 0`. Terrain height still isn't *gameplay*-relevant (movement speed, sightlines) — that stays deliberately deferred until something actually needs it.
- **Step 7 — Skills and knowledge** (partial): resources, skills, and techniques are data-driven rather than compiled — each is defined by a JSON file (`src/ManyWinters.Godot/Content/{resources,skills}/<id>/<id>.json`) loaded at startup into a `ResourceCatalog`/`SkillCatalog`, keyed by string ids (`ResourceKindId`/`SkillTypeId`/`TechniqueId`). Each resource's definition names the skill it trains — apples and pears both train `foraging`, mushrooms and potatoes each train their own skill, so gathering-style skills don't have to be 1:1 with resource kinds. `Person.Skills` grows per skill with each gather action, discovering that skill's efficient technique after enough practice — bigger harvests afterward, and the discovery doesn't leak into other skills. Practice has diminishing returns (`Skills.Increase` divides each gain by the level already held, so a level grows as the square root of the practice behind it): a flat point per action made a level a tally of repetitions, and repetitions are cheap — a person parked at one apple tree reached level 193 over a single simulated year. Every discovery threshold is therefore written as a number of tries (`Skills.LevelAfter(5)`) rather than as a bare level, so the pacing — the fifth try is the one that discovers — survives any retuning of the curve. Right-click one person onto another to `TeachCommand` them anything the first person knows. Knowledge lives in people, not a global tech tree, and it's all shown in the Godot info panel. A full prerequisite graph is still deferred.
- **Step 6 — Headless + visual milestone**: `SurvivalMilestoneTests` (`src/ManyWinters.Tests/Milestones/`) proves 10–20 people survive 300 ticks with regular gathering and starve within 150 ticks without it. Written before seasons existed; that 300-tick run now spans a full year including one winter (see Step 9), so it doubles as a basic winter-survival check. The Godot scene now spawns 15 people and 6 resource nodes to match.
- **Step 8 — Tools and construction**: item instances exist as an `Inventory` (`ManyWinters.Core.Items`) — a per-person one, keyed by `ItemKindId`. Gathering a resource whose definition sets `yieldsItem` (currently only `wood`) fills the inventory instead of relieving hunger; the existing food resources are unchanged. A `SkillDefinition` can name a `tool` item and a harvest bonus for it — carrying an `axe` while woodcutting harvests more per action, on top of the Step 7 technique bonus. Crafting is data-driven too: `RecipeCatalog` (`src/ManyWinters.Godot/Content/recipes/<id>/<id>.json`) maps an output item to its input item/amount, and `CraftCommand` consumes the input from a person's inventory to produce it — wired to a "Craft Axe" button in the Godot UI. Construction follows the same shape: `Building` is a `WorldState`-tracked entity (`ManyWinters.Core.Construction`), `BuildingCatalog` defines what a building costs to put up (currently one `storage_hut`, 20 wood), and `ConstructCommand` consumes the materials and adds the building — wired to a "Build Storage Hut" button. Adding this whole new entity type only touched `WorldPresenter` (a `BuildingAdded` event + a `BuildingView`), not `Main`, validating the Step-8-detour presenter refactor. `Building.Condition` decays slowly every tick and `RepairCommand` restores it for a quarter of the build cost, wired to a "Repair Nearest Building" button. A `Building` also has its own `Inventory` — the same type a `Person` uses — so `storage_hut` is a genuine shared store rather than just a name: `DepositCommand`/`WithdrawCommand` move items between a person and the nearest building, wired to "Deposit Wood"/"Withdraw Wood" buttons, letting people pool resources instead of only using what they personally gathered. Entities also get deterministic per-instance tint/scale variation (`EntityVisualVariation`, seeded by entity id) so repeats of the same kind don't render as identical clones. Visuals themselves are billboard sprites (`BillboardSprite`) with real pixel art under `Content/{people,resources,buildings}/`, generated by `art/generate_sprites.py`.
- **Step 9 — Seasons and first winter** (partial): `WorldState.CurrentSeason` cycles Spring→Summer→Autumn→Winter derived from tick count (75 ticks/season, shown in the Godot UI next to the tick counter) — no extra state to save/load since it's computed straight from the clock. Gameplay effects (hunger cost, resource yield) never key off the `Season` name directly, though — that would hardcode "Winter is harsh," which doesn't generalize (a southern hemisphere would have it backwards). Instead, a `SeasonParameters` maps `Season → Climate` (`Cold`/`Mild`/`Hot`) and `Climate → hunger multiplier` / `Climate → regen multiplier`; swapping which season is `Cold` is a different `SeasonParameters` instance, not a code change, and there's no `if (climate == ...)` branch anywhere — both hunger accrual and resource regrowth are just `baseRate * multiplierForClimate`, uniformly, every tick. Each `ResourceDefinition` similarly carries a `ClimateYields` list — per-climate yield multipliers — so a resource can thrive in one climate and struggle in another (the four food resources drop to 40% yield in `Cold`, `wood` is unaffected) without any resource-specific branching in `GatherCommand`. `ResourceCatalog`/`SkillCatalog`/`RecipeCatalog`/`BuildingCatalog`/`ItemCatalog`/`SeasonParameters` now bundle into one `WorldConfiguration` passed into `WorldState`, replacing what had grown into four separate constructor overloads. Resource nodes also regrow — each `ResourceDefinition` can set a `RegenPerTick`, capped at the amount a node was originally spawned with (`MaxAmount`), scaled to zero in `Cold` via the regen multiplier (default calendar) rather than a special-cased pause — so a node survives being harvested across multiple winters instead of only ever depleting. Clothing/insulation closes the loop: a new `ItemCatalog` (`Content/items/<id>/<id>.json`) gives items metadata beyond being bare inventory keys — `warm_clothing` (crafted from 10 wood, same shape as the axe) carries an `Insulation` value that reduces a person's *effective* hunger multiplier (`Max(1, baseMultiplier - insulation)`, floored at the normal rate, never a bonus) — so surviving winter well is a product of prior preparation, not just luck. `WinterSurvivalMilestoneTests` proves winter's pressure is real (stopping gathering right as it begins is fatal within it) as distinct from Step 6's "never gather at all" baseline. Actual per-location climate (hemispheres, biomes) needs a concept of place that doesn't exist yet, so it's deferred alongside weather severity.
- **Step 10 — Life, death, and continuity**: a `Person` now has a `BirthTick`, and `WorldState.AgeInYears`/`AgeInSeasons` derive their age from it the same way `CurrentSeason` derives from the clock (10 years — `SimulationRules.MaxLifespanYears` — is deliberately short given the game's 300-tick year, so old age is observable within a normal play/test session); dying of old age sits alongside the existing hunger death, both recorded via `DeathTick` and now a `CauseOfDeath` (`Hunger`/`OldAge`, old age taking priority if both conditions are met on the same tick). The inspector shows age in whole winters once someone's had their first one, or in seasons before that (matching the game's own title). Every `Person` has a `Mother` and a `Father` (never null: a starting person with no recorded parent gets a dead forebear from `WorldState.Forebears`, and every line ends at `Person.Unknown`) — `MapLoader.LoadDefault` hand-assigns the ties among the starting band (three couples, two children each), and every tie made after the story begins comes from `BirthCommand` (below). Dying doesn't move a person's possessions anywhere — they just stay in the corpse's own `Inventory`, lying with the body at its position rather than teleporting anywhere. `LootCommand` — proximity-gated exactly like burial — is how anyone living, not specifically a relative, walks up and physically takes them; there's no rightful-heir check, so possessions go to whoever gets there first, matching how a body's belongings work physically rather than by legal succession. Burial itself is *not* an automatic side effect of death — a deceased person just lies there, unburied, until someone deliberately buries them. Burial reuses the Step 7 skill/technique mechanic: a `burial` skill (`Content/skills/burial/burial.json`) grows with practice via `BuryCommand` and eventually discovers `efficient_burial`; burying without that technique produces an anonymous, unmarked `Grave` (no name, no age, no cause of death, no parents, no preserved knowledge — the link to who the person was is genuinely severed, not just hidden), while burying with it produces a fully recorded one, now also naming the cause of death and (when known) the deceased's mother and father — snapshotted as plain names at burial time, not live references, so the record stays meaningful even after a named parent is later buried too. Wired to a "Bury Nearest Dead Person" button and a "Loot Nearest Dead Person" button, a live grave count, and a clickable `GraveView` billboard (tinted differently for marked vs. unmarked, `res://Content/graves/grave_{marked,unmarked}.png` — no art drawn yet, so it falls back to a flat tinted quad like every other kind did before its sprites existed) that swaps the inspector panel over to that grave's record (or "Unmarked grave - no record survives" for anonymous ones).

  Children are born, not only inherited from the starting band. `BirthCommand` adds one to two living adults (`WorldState.IsOldEnoughForChildren`, i.e. `LifeStages.AdultAgeYears` and up — elders included, since this is a floor on childhood rather than a fertility model) standing within reach of each other, at the mother's position, carrying nothing: no skills, no techniques, no belongings. Knowledge here is taught, never inherited, so a child born to two expert foragers starts exactly as ignorant as one born to anybody else — what it ends up knowing is whatever `TeachCommand` and `WorldState.AutoTeachNearbyPeople` manage to pass it, and both of those check reach.

  That is what makes the rest of it matter. An infant (`LifeStage.Infant`, under `LifeStages.WeaningAgeYears`) does not get hungry at all while its mother is alive and beside it, and its idle behaviour is neither foraging nor wandering but a `FollowTask` that keeps it at her side — so the child that stays close is also the child that gets taught, and the one that ends up alone gets neither. What the feeding costs turns up on her side of the same tick as `SimulationRules.NursingHungerMultiplier`, deliberately a cost on the mother rather than a transfer between the two: a transfer would depend on which of the pair `Advance` happened to reach first, whereas both halves of this read the same facts independently. A mother already nursing cannot have a second child until the first is weaned, and an orphaned infant genuinely starves — nothing else in the world will feed it, and a nursing mother with nothing to eat is herself on a noticeably shorter clock than usual.

  `LifeStage`/`LifeStages` name the bands of a life (infant, child, adult, elder) in one place, and `CarryCapacity`'s curve now turns at those same ages rather than at private constants of its own — it is one body growing up and then tiring, not two curves that happen to look alike. Also deliberately deferred: age does not affect walking speed (`MoveCommand` still uses one number for everybody), and a newborn is drawn at the same size as an adult, since `EntityVisualVariation` seeds scale off the entity id and knows nothing about age.

  Children then stopped needing to be asked for. `Affections` is the first thing in the game that remembers something about a *pair* of people rather than about a person — casual teaching already looks at pairs but deliberately keeps no state, its roll being a pure function of the two ids and the tick. A bond grows every tick two living people spend within `SimulationRules.TogetherDistance` of each other and fades every tick they do not (`AffectionGainedPerTickTogether` an order of magnitude above `AffectionLostPerTickApart`, so a friendship is made faster than it is lost and someone away on a long errand comes back to the people they knew). It is symmetric and stored once per pair rather than once per direction: real fondness is not mutual, but time spent together is, and one number per pair is half the state to grow, decay and save. Bonds involving the dead are skipped rather than decayed, so what someone meant to the people around them is still there to read afterwards.

  Once a bond passes `AffectionNeededToHaveAChild`, `WorldState.Advance` issues the `BirthCommand` itself. Everything about whether a birth is *possible* stayed in the command rather than being repeated in the pass, so the player's button and the world's own pass can never disagree: both alive, both grown, one of each sex, not close kin, within reach, and the mother not already nursing. That last one is also what stops a devoted couple producing a child every tick — the next one waits until the first is weaned. A newborn starts at `StartingAffectionWithParents` with each parent (`docs/todo/todo.md`, "u potomku automaticky vyšší"), deliberately well below the reproduction threshold, and `Kinship` rules out parents, children and siblings outright, so closeness to family can never be the kind that makes more family. `Person.Unknown` is explicitly not a shared parent there, or the whole starting band would count as one family.

  This needed a `Sex` on `Person`, which is as narrow as it sounds: nothing about anyone's work, strength, knowledge or standing reads it. It is `required`, like `Mother` and `Father` and for the same reason — there is no such thing as a person without one, so nobody leaves it to chance by accident. A caller with no opinion asks for a draw out loud with `Person.SexOf(id)`, which spreads one bit of the id through `SeedHash` the way every other per-entity variation in this game does (`EntityVisualVariation`, an idle wander, a casual-teaching roll); `SpawnPersonCommand` and `BirthCommand` both do exactly that, and `MapLoader` instead names one, because its starting band comes with a family table that has already settled who bore whom. It briefly had a nullable backing field meaning "unset" so that the forty-odd existing construction sites did not have to be touched, which quietly made every unstated test person's sex a fresh coin flip per run — the first `BirthCommandTests` run failed because "Sela" came out male. Requiring it cost about thirty mechanical lines in tests (`TestPeople.AnySex` names the ones with no stake in the answer) and removed that whole class of flake. The sex is saved rather than re-derived on load, since a chosen one would otherwise be replaced by whatever the id says; the save format is at version 16, with the bonds alongside it.

  `FamilyMilestoneTests` is what keeps the tuning honest, and it earned its place immediately: the first version of the affection radius was arm's reach, every unit test passed, and no child was ever born in the actual game. It also turned up something that has nothing to do with families — the shipped starting band, left entirely alone, is dead inside a hundred ticks, because nobody is born knowing how to eat and the player is the one expected to teach them. That milestone therefore hands the band eating and foraging and stops short of the first winter, which kills it for the separate reason `WinterSurvivalMilestoneTests` already covers.

  Still open, and it was `docs/todo/todo.md`'s own question: whether reproduction should have to be taught like eating and burial are. It does not today — the affection is enough, and the "Have Child With Nearest Person" button remains as the player's direct way to make one happen.
